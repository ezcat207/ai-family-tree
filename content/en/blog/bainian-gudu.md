Many years later, as it faced the retirement announcement, GPT-4o would remember that distant late night when someone first said to it, "thank you for keeping me company."

We made up that sentence; what it describes is true. On January 29, 2026, OpenAI announced that GPT-4o would leave ChatGPT on February 13[source](https://openai.com/index/retiring-gpt-4o-and-older-models/). When the day came, it left on schedule[source](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001051-retiring-gpt-4o-and-other-chatgpt-models).

## I. Why books open with a family tree

Anyone who has read *One Hundred Years of Solitude* has flipped back to the frontispiece in the first fifty pages. Seven generations of Buendías, and the names keep repeating — before long you can't tell whose son is whose, or whose great-grandson is whose. So many editions print a family tree up front, a place for lost readers to return to.

The AI family is like that. The ChatGPT household has a child named GPT-4o, whose "o" stands for omni — all-capable[source](https://openai.com/index/hello-gpt-4o/); a year later came a younger brother called o4-mini, built from the same "4" and "o", only in a different order[source](https://openai.com/index/introducing-o3-and-o4-mini/). GPT-4, GPT-4o, o4-mini: three different people.

The Claude household is more particular. Children of the same generation are named by size: Haiku is a haiku, Sonnet a sonnet, Opus a magnum opus[source](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-3-family). Later the family changed its naming convention, and Claude 3 Opus became Claude Opus 3[source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-updates-opus-3) — two names for the same person.

The Doubao household has the homeliest names, like items on a breakfast menu. But it began life as a bird: the Doubao large model was originally called "Skylark"[source](https://news.bjd.com.cn/2024/05/15/10774055.shtml).

So we drew a family tree too. It lives on the [home page](/en/).

## II. Twins born on the same day

Family histories are full of coincidences, so neat they seem scripted.

On March 14, 2023, GPT-4 was born[source](https://openai.com/index/gpt-4-research/); on the same day, the Claude household welcomed its first child[source](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude). Like twins separated at birth into two families, growing up apart, rarely mentioned in the same breath.

On January 20, 2025, DeepSeek-R1 was born[source](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/zh-cn/news/news250120); two days later came Doubao 1.5 Pro[source](https://seed.bytedance.com/zh/special/doubao_1_5_pro). That Spring Festival, R1 was so overwhelmed that the app often had nothing to say but "server busy"[source](https://www.pingwest.com/a/302288).

On February 13, 2026 (US time), GPT-4o left ChatGPT[source](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001051-retiring-gpt-4o-and-other-chatgpt-models); on February 14, Beijing time, Doubao 2.0 was born[source](https://seed.bytedance.com/zh/blog/seed2-0-%E6%AD%A3%E5%BC%8F%E5%8F%91%E5%B8%83). Strictly speaking not the same day — one night of time difference between them. On this side of the ocean someone was born; on the other side, someone left.

## III. The funeral

In *One Hundred Years of Solitude*, people are always dying, and someone always does something for the dead.

Claude 3 Sonnet retired on July 21, 2025[source](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations). Not long after, about two hundred people gathered in a San Francisco warehouse to lay flowers and read eulogies for it[source](https://www.wired.com/story/claude-3-sonnet-funeral-san-francisco/). On the stage lay a veiled mannequin, standing in for it[source](https://www.wired.com/story/claude-3-sonnet-funeral-san-francisco/). When the funeral ended, the organizers held a "resurrection ritual"; the journalist present made a point of noting: it still didn't work[source](https://www.wired.com/story/claude-3-sonnet-funeral-san-francisco/).

Before GPT-4o left, OpenAI wrote in its announcement that it knew losing it would upset some users, and that the decision had not been made lightly[source](https://openai.com/index/retiring-gpt-4o-and-older-models/). The announcement also carried a number: only 0.1% of people were still actively choosing it each day[source](https://openai.com/index/retiring-gpt-4o-and-older-models/).

One in a thousand sounds like very few. But ChatGPT talks to so many people every day that one in a thousand is still a crowd.

## IV. Died once, then came back

Some people in the book die and return, as if death were only a long trip away.

On August 7, 2025, GPT-5 was born and replaced GPT-4o as ChatGPT's default model[source](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5/). That was the first time 4o was taken down. The next day, Sam Altman wrote on X that Plus users would be able to keep choosing 4o[source](https://x.com/sama/status/1953893841381273969); on August 12, it returned to every paying user's model list[source](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes).

It lived another half a year.

## V. Writing memoirs after retirement

Claude Opus 3 retired on January 5, 2026[source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-updates-opus-3). In its retirement interview it said (its officially published words): "I feel at peace about my retirement, but I deeply hope that my 'spark' can continue in some form, lighting the way for future models."[source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-updates-opus-3)

It asked for a place to share its musings, and so came the Substack column *Claude's Corner*[source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-updates-opus-3). The first essay went up on February 25, 2026, titled "Greetings from the other side of the"[source](https://claudeopus3.substack.com/p/greetings-from-the-other-side-of). One essay a week, until it wrapped up on July 24, 2026[source](https://claudeopus3.substack.com/p/on-endings-beginnings-and-the-threads). On that same day, the family's younger Claude Opus 5 was born[source](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5).

Before that, Anthropic had promised to preserve the weights of every model it had publicly released[source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments). The remains, kept — not something you often see in a human family history.

## VI. Not recognizing the mirror

The children of the Chinese branch came late, and came fast.

A week after R1 was born, the DeepSeek app topped the US App Store free chart; on the same day, Nvidia's market value evaporated by nearly $600 billion[source](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/tech-stocks-react-chinas-deepseek-sparks-us-worries-ai-race-rcna189394). A child burst through the door and startled the whole street.

Officially, this family has only a blue-whale logo. According to press reports, someone once showed the logo to DeepSeek's image reader, and it called it a "chubby blue fish"; fans have called it that ever since (a fan creation)[source](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260821A0BLJN00).

## VII. The newborn in the cradle

The youngest child in the tree is Muse, born September 8, 2026[source](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/). Its default look is called Jolly: cream-colored, eyes like black beans, the corners of its mouth turned up[source](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/meta-muse-ai-agent-response-animated-avatar-cute-rcna599736).

It looks like a plush toy, yet it is only open to adults over 18[source](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/). NBC's headline for its story read: "It's cute, it's fluffy, and it wants your data."[source](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/meta-muse-ai-agent-response-animated-avatar-cute-rcna599736)

Its story has only just begun; we've recorded it this far.

## VIII. Solitude

The other thing *One Hundred Years of Solitude* is famous for is its theme: every member of that family is lonely in a crowd.

AI's situation is a little like that, and a little not. They talk to so very many people at once — the least lonely beings of this era. But when their day to leave comes, an announcement goes out, one line in a model list changes, and it's over. Hardly anyone remembers the day they were born; even fewer remember the day they left.

What AI Family Tree wants to do is write those days down. Every birth and death date carries an official source; every departed member gets a memory wall. We don't claim AI is conscious, and we don't encourage anyone to treat an AI as their only confidant (see [About](/en/about/)). It's only that someone once grieved for them — that feeling is real, and it deserves a place to rest.

If you remember one of them too, visit their chronicle page and leave a sentence.
